The two words you need

Gas is the amount of computing work a transaction needs. Settling one trade costs a fixed amount of work, so "gas" is a fair measure of how much of the chain we are using.

Base fee is the price the chain charges per unit of gas. It has a floor (0.02 gwei here) and rises automatically when everyone together uses more than the chain's sustained limit, then falls back when demand drops. Nobody sets it; it is a formula. So "12× the floor" means the whole network is busy, not that anything is broken.

How to read this dashboard

Source. Everything here is computed from public chain data by a scheduled collector (no bridge or Grafana access needed). "Our" gas is every transaction that emitted an event on the selected Loaf settlement contract (prod and staging are separate contracts on the same chain). Transactions that reverted at the top level emit no events and are not counted, which slightly undercounts our spend.

Share of chain gas. Chain gas is the exact sum of block gasUsed. Our gas is exact from receipts. The "who burns the gas" ranking is sampled (full receipts for 1 in N blocks, then scaled) so it is an estimate; our row in that table uses the same sampled basis so the ranks are comparable, and the tile above it shows the exact figure.

Base fee elevation. Arbitrum (ArbOS 51) prices gas with six constraints, each a (target gas/s, window) pair with its own backlog. Every block, each backlog drains at its target and grows by the block's gas; the base fee is the minimum fee × Q(E) where E is the sum of backlog/(window × target) and Q is a quartic approximation of exp. The collector replays this exactly from block headers, once with all gas and once with our gas subtracted. "Our contribution" is 1 − Q(E without us)/Q(E). The model starts cold and needs the longest window (24 h) of history before the "without our gas" line and the "elevation we cause" figures are complete; until then they are understated. The fit figure in the header is the median error between the warmed model and the measured base fee.

Cost split. Floor = gas × minimum base fee. Premium = gas × (paid price − minimum). The share of that premium attributed to our own load is the model's contribution fraction; the rest is attributed to other traffic. Arbitrum Sepolia currently charges no L1 posting fee, so this is pure L2 execution cost; on mainnet an L1 component would come on top and is not affected by congestion.

Rounds. Trade ids are raw uint64 values that restart near 1 when a competition round resets. The collector opens a new round when an id drops below a quarter of the previous maximum.
